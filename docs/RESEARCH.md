# TIMELINER — Research basis

Why each game mechanic exists. **Rule:** a mechanic ships only if it maps to a finding here or to real user feedback in `docs/FEEDBACK.md`.

Evidence strength: ●●● meta-analysis or widely replicated · ●● solid studies · ● single study or expert practice.

## 1. Findings → mechanics

| # | Finding | Evidence | What TIMELINER does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Implementation intentions.** "When X happens, I will do Y" plans greatly raise follow-through compared with plain goals. | Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, meta-analysis of 94 studies, d ≈ 0.65 ●●● | Putting an item at a time on the timeline *is* an if-then plan. The add sheet shows the sentence "At 3:45 PM, in Work, I will …". Planning ahead earns a small XP. |
| 2 | **Planning fallacy.** People underestimate task length, even when they know past tasks ran over. | Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994 ●●● | Track planned vs. actual time for every task. Review shows your personal overrun factor and suggests padding. Bonus XP when actual is within ±20 % of plan: we reward *honest* plans, not *big* plans. |
| 3 | **Goal gradient.** Effort speeds up as the goal gets near. | Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006 ●● | Every task, day, week, month and year has a progress bar. "2 tasks to level 6" hints. The now-line visibly approaches the end of the current task. |
| 4 | **Endowed progress.** A head start raises completion (car-wash cards: 34 % vs 19 %). | Nunes & Drèze 2006 ●● | Planning the day counts as its first step: the day's path starts with the planning node already filled. |
| 5 | **Progress principle.** Small visible wins are the strongest daily motivator at work (≈12,000 diary entries). | Amabile & Kramer 2011 ●● | Instant feedback on every completion (card checks, character steps forward, haptic). Review lists wins first. |
| 6 | **Open loops.** Unfinished goals keep pulling attention; making a concrete plan for them stops that. | Masicampo & Baumeister 2011 ●● | Two-tap capture into an Inbox lane. Focus mode has "park this thought". Review ends with "place tomorrow's first task". |
| 7 | **Switching costs and attention residue.** Switching tasks costs time; attention stays stuck on the previous task. | Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans 2001 ●●; Leroy 2009 ●● | Focus mode shows one task only. Small todos can be batched into one task block. The app sends nothing during a focus block except its end. |
| 8 | **Breaks.** Short breaks raise energy and lower fatigue; fixed-interval breaks lowered fatigue more than self-chosen ones. | Albulescu et al. 2022, meta-analysis ●●●; Biwer et al. 2023 ● | Break blocks are first-class and earn XP like work. After a task longer than 45 min the app suggests a 5-minute break. |
| 9 | **Habits form slowly.** Median 66 days (range 18–254); missing one day did not reset the habit. | Lally et al. 2010 ●● | Streaks are forgiving: earned streak freezes, optional weekly target (e.g., 5 of 7 days), never shame. |
| 10 | **"What-the-hell" effect.** After one slip, people often drop the whole goal. | Polivy & Herman 1985 ●● | A broken streak offers a "repair" quest instead of a reset-to-zero splash. Skipped tasks become neutral grey gaps, not red failures. |
| 11 | **Loss aversion.** Losses feel about twice as strong as gains. | Kahneman & Tversky 1979 ●●● | Used gently: one evening reminder when a streak is at risk. Earned XP and items are never taken away. |
| 12 | **Fresh-start effect.** People start goals at time landmarks (Monday, 1st of the month, New Year, birthday). | Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014 ●● | Week/month/year zoom highlights landmarks. On a landmark the app offers "set a goal for this week / month / year". |
| 13 | **Self-determination theory.** Lasting motivation needs autonomy, competence and relatedness; good games feed all three. | Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006 ●●; Deci & Ryan 2000 ●●● | Autonomy: user picks layers, zoom, direction and their own treats. Competence: levels and per-layer skill. Relatedness: party timelines with friends (later). |
| 14 | **Overjustification.** Expected, tangible rewards can lower intrinsic motivation; informational feedback does not. | Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, meta-analysis ●●● | XP is *feedback* ("you focused 3 h today"), not payment. Rewards are cosmetic world growth. No paywalled rewards. |
| 15 | **Gamification works, modestly, and depends on design.** | Hamari, Koivisto & Sarsa 2014 ●●; Sailer & Homner 2020, meta-analysis: cognitive g ≈ 0.49, motivational g ≈ 0.36, behavioural g ≈ 0.25 ●●● | The game is a layer over a good planner, never a replacement. A "calm mode" turns the game layer down. |
| 16 | **Leaderboards can backfire.** A class with badges and leaderboards showed lower intrinsic motivation and exam scores. | Hanus & Fox 2015 ●● | No public leaderboards. Co-op party goals instead of competition. |
| 17 | **Temptation bundling.** Pairing a "want" with a "should" increases the "should". | Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014 ●● | Optional "treat" on a task (podcast, playlist, coffee), shown when the task starts. |
| 18 | **Mental contrasting (WOOP).** Imagining the obstacle plus an if-then plan beats positive thinking alone for big goals. | Oettingen 2014; Duckworth et al. 2011 ●● | Month and year goals use a four-field form: Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan. |
| 19 | **Peak-end rule.** We judge an experience by its peak and its end. | Kahneman et al. 1993 ●● | The end-of-day Review ends on the day's best moment, never on a list of misses. |
| 20 | **Seeing time pass** helps time awareness, especially with ADHD. | Visual timers are standard ADHD support; evidence is mostly practice-based ● | The live, flowing now-line and filling progress bars are the core UI, not an extra. |

## 2. Popular apps: what to borrow, what to avoid

| App | Borrow | Avoid / their gap |
|---|---|---|
| Structured | Visual day timeline, colorful blocks, inbox | Day view only: no smooth zoom out to week → year |
| Tiimo | Calm visual planning for neurodivergent users, timers | Little game layer |
| Forest | Focus session grows something visible | The tree *dies* if you leave the app (punishment) |
| Habitica | Full RPG around tasks | HP loss for misses; heavy UI; people quit after slips |
| Finch | Kind tone; a pet that grows with self-care | Not a planner |
| Duolingo | Streak, streak freeze, daily goal | Notification pressure, leaderboard stress |
| Todoist | Fast capture, recurring tasks, Karma levels | List only, no sense of time |
| TickTick | Pomodoro + calendar + habits in one app | Crowded features |
| Sunsama | Daily planning and shutdown ritual | Work-only, expensive |
| Google / Notion Calendar | Time grid, drag to create | No motivation layer |
| Apple Fitness rings | Closing rings = goal gradient in one glance | — |
| Time Timer | Shrinking disk shows remaining time | — |
| "Your Life in Weeks" (Tim Urban) | Zooming out to years gives perspective | — |

**Where TIMELINER is different:** one continuous timeline from seconds to years with a live now-line, parallel layers, any direction, and a forgiving game layer built on the findings above.

## 3. Anti-patterns we will not ship
- **Punishment for misses** (HP loss, dying trees). It triggers the what-the-hell effect (#10).
- **Loot boxes or random paid rewards.** Gambling-like. Pro sells convenience and cosmetics only.
- **Rewarding task count.** People farm tiny fake tasks. We reward planned time completed and good estimates, and cap planning XP per day.
- **Notification spam.** At most one nudge per day that the user did not set up themselves.
- **Public comparison** (#16).

## 4. How we check it works (own data, anonymous)
- **Follow-through:** % of planned tasks completed, per week.
- **Calibration:** median actual ÷ planned duration; should move toward 1.0.
- **Comeback rate:** % of users active 7 days after a streak break (the what-the-hell metric).
- **Retention:** D1 / D7 / D30.

Counts and ratios only. Never titles, notes or other personal content (see privacy rule in CLAUDE.md).
